Four of these ten are already over

Fastly's CDN failed for 49 minutes in June 2021. CrowdStrike cancelled 5,078 flights in July 2024. Those are over. Meanwhile one county in Virginia still holds the control plane for every AWS customer on earth, 49% of domains still resolve through ten providers, and the CLOUD Act still reaches inside Microsoft's EU Data Boundary. Four of these ten are finished events; six are conditions that have not stopped — and the record marks neither.

Qualification

“Severity” is not measuring severity

The field is called severity and takes two values. Neither is a magnitude. They answer a different question — has this already happened? — and sorting by them ranks nothing.

severity: demonstrated — 4 cases

“Demonstrated failure”. An event with a date, a duration and a casualty list.
  • AWS December 2021 outage cascade · 7 hr
  • CrowdStrike July 2024 · —
  • Fastly CDN outage · 49 min
  • AWS October 2025 · 15 hr

severity: structural — 6 cases

“Structural dependency”. A standing arrangement with no event, no duration, and no end.
  • AWS us-east-1 control plane concentration
  • EU data sovereignty
  • Loudoun County
  • Epic on Azure/AWS
  • DNS concentration
  • Cloud egress pricing
OBSERVED the field values, verbatim. NOT DERIVED any ordering — a 49-minute CDN failure and a control-plane concentration with no recorded end are not two points on one scale, and this page does not place them on one.

A demonstrated failure is better evidenced than a structural one — it has a date, a duration, and named casualties. A structural dependency is arguably the more serious finding and the harder one to prove. Reading the field as severity gets the relationship backwards: it is an axis of proof, not of harm.

AWS us-east-1 control plane concentration
Control plane Structural dependency
The dependencyAWS Identity & Access Management (IAM), Route 53 DNS management, CloudFront provisioning, AWS Organizations, and DynamoDB Global Tables all have control planes that require us-east-1. Customers deploying in eu-west-1 or ap-southeast-1 still depend on us-east-1 for authentication, DNS changes, and account management. What happened / what it costsWhen us-east-1 fails, services in other regions can't authenticate, can't update DNS, can't provision new resources. The December 2021 outage proved this: services not explicitly using us-east-1 failed because their control plane dependencies lived there. Who decidesAmazon Web Services. $35B invested in Northern Virginia alone. 65 data centers in Loudoun County. $576M in annual local tax revenue (30%+ of county budget). Same county driving PJM capacity cost explosion. Who is downstreamAll AWS customers globally

Outage cascade Demonstrated failure

Dec 22: us-east-1 power failure took down Slack, Okta, NY Court System. What happened / what it costsBillions in estimated economic losses. Ring doorbells stopped working. Robinhood trading halted. IRS website went down. NY state court filing system offline. Each outage revealed new dependency chains nobody had mapped. Who decidesAWS. Customers had no visibility into the control plane dependency until it failed. Post-incident, AWS published guidance on reducing us-east-1 dependencies — effectively admitting the concentration risk they'd built. Who is downstreamDisney+, Netflix, Slack, Robinhood, Coinbase, Ticketmaster, Venmo, Instacart, Roku, Kindle, IRS, NY Court System
CrowdStrike July 2024
Software supply chain Demonstrated failure
The dependencyCrowdStrike Falcon runs at kernel level on Windows systems. Channel file 291 update caused boot loop. Every organization running CrowdStrike on Windows was affected simultaneously. No customer action triggered it. What happened / what it costs5,078 flights cancelled (4.6% of scheduled). Hospitals reverted to paper records. Emergency dispatch systems degraded. Who is downstream8.5M Windows systems. Airlines (5,078 flights cancelled), banks, hospitals, stock markets, gas stations, emergency services.
Fastly CDN outage
CDN concentration Demonstrated failure
The dependencyFastly CDN serves as the edge layer between users and origin servers. Bug deployed May 12 lay dormant until triggered June 8 by a valid config change. 85% of Fastly's network returned errors simultaneously. What happened / what it costsMajor news, government, e-commerce, and social platforms unreachable for ~49 minutes. NYT redirected to Google Cloud origin as workaround. UK government services offline. The bug was in Fastly's own deployment from a month earlier. Every Fastly customer inherited the risk. AWS October 2025
Outage cascade Demonstrated failure
The dependencyDynamoDB automation had a race condition that deleted DNS records in us-east-1 — not a Route 53 failure, but an internal automation error that cascaded through DNS. 141 of AWS's own services affected. Ring, smart locks, thermostats, and IoT devices with no offline fallback went dark. What happened / what it costs10 million Ring devices offline — no video recording, no motion alerts, no remote access. Smart locks unresponsive. 4M end users affected across 1,000+ companies. Demonstrated IoT's total cloud dependency: devices become inert hardware when the connection drops. Who decidesAWS. Ring is an Amazon subsidiary — even Amazon's own hardware products couldn't survive Amazon's own infrastructure failure. Consumers who bought 'security' devices found their security was downstream of a DNS table. Who is downstream4M users, 1,000+ companies, 141 AWS services. Ring doorbells (10M devices offline), smart locks, thermostats.
EU data sovereignty
Sovereignty gap Structural dependency
The dependency120+ countries now have data protection laws. EU GDPR requires data residency. But the US CLOUD Act (2018) compels US-based providers to produce data regardless of storage location. Microsoft's EU Data Boundary addresses residency but explicitly does not override CLOUD Act obligations. What happened / what it costsGaia-X — the EU's sovereign cloud initiative — was captured by US hyperscalers who lobbied for inclusion and diluted sovereignty requirements. EU organizations using AWS/Azure/GCP have data residency but not data sovereignty. 62% of global cloud market controlled by three US companies. Who decidesUS government (via CLOUD Act) and US hyperscalers (via market dominance). EU Data Boundary is a geographic feature, not a legal one. Executive agreements with UK (2019) and Australia (2021) formalize the access mechanism. Who is downstreamEU governments, enterprises, and citizens using US cloud providers

Physical concentration Structural dependency
The dependency115 data centers in one county. AWS alone has 65 (500+ acres). Data centers drove 63% of PJM's $9.3B capacity cost increase and 94% of projected 32GW demand growth. Epic on Azure/AWS
Sector dependency Structural dependency
The dependencyEpic Systems dominates US hospital EHR market. 30 health systems now run Epic on public cloud (90% AWS, some Azure). Epic stopped Google Cloud integrations, narrowing the vendor pool to two. Healthcare cloud market: $79.5B (2024) → $309.5B (2033). What happened / what it costsPatient records, clinical workflows, medication ordering, and billing all run through one EHR vendor on one or two cloud providers. DNS concentration
CDN / DNS concentration Structural dependency
The dependencyDNS is the phone book of the internet. 49% of all domains depend on Top 10 NS providers. 98% of domains use a single NS provider — no redundancy. 5% of indirect resolvers support 90% of all forwarding. BGP routing errors cascade through DNS: one misconfiguration can make entire networks unreachable. What happened / what it costsFacebook October 2021: BGP routing error → authoritative DNS failure → 3.5 billion users offline for 6 hours. Internal tools also went down, preventing engineers from fixing it remotely. Demonstrated that DNS concentration creates systemic risk invisible to users. Who decidesA handful of DNS/CDN providers. No single authority verifies BGP route announcements. Domain owners choose one NS provider and inherit its failure modes. End users have no visibility into which DNS provider serves the sites they use. Who is downstream49% of all domain names. 90% of DNS forwarding through 5% of resolvers.
Cloud egress pricing
Pricing as governance Structural dependency
The dependency$0.08-0.12/GB to move data out. 6-12% of typical cloud bills (20-40% for data-heavy orgs). Cross-region pricing incentivizes co-location in one region (reducing resilience). Reserved instances lock organizations into multi-year commitments. 2025: providers reduced egress fees under regulatory pressure, but cross-AZ/NAT gateway costs unchanged. What happened / what it costsOrganizations cannot afford to leave — migration cost exceeds switching benefit. Architecture decisions driven by billing rather than resilience. Cross-AZ pricing discourages the very redundancy that would protect against outages. The pricing model enforces concentration. Who decidesAWS, Azure, GCP set the prices. 62% of global cloud market controlled by three companies. Cloud market at $419B/year (2025), growing 25% YoY. Organizations report vendor lock-in as top concern but cannot afford to act on it. Who is downstreamAll organizations using hyperscaler cloud services

Audit · what the marks mean
● Observed
Read off a cited source. Has a unit and a receipt.
◌ Derived
Computed here. Shows its rule; carries no receipt.
▌ Interpreted
Authored judgment. Publishable — and not evidence.
⊘ Refused
Declined or unavailable. Shown, not omitted.
What this page does not claim. That any of these outages will recur, or that a structural dependency will fail. That one case is worse than another. That the four dated events are representative of anything — they are the ones somebody wrote about.
Audit · boundary conditions

What this page does not show

An atlas of ongoing dependency, whose only precise records are of things that stopped.
  • Every date on this page is inferred. The schema has no date field, so event dates are the earliest citation attached to the case. A citation can predate or postdate the thing it describes; these are proxies and should not be read as a record.
  • Durations come from prose, not fields. 3 of the 4 events state one somewhere in their own text. Nothing enforces that they are measured the same way.
  • Selection is invisible. Four outages appear out of the many that occurred in this period. The corpus records no criterion for inclusion, so the set cannot be read as a sample of anything.
  • The conditions are not unmeasured — they are undated. Their prose carries plenty of figures (49% of domains, 115 data centers, 8.5 million machines). What none of them has is a date, a duration, or a field to put either in. The schema has no slot for when a standing arrangement began.
  • The event/condition split is the corpus's, and it leaks. The DNS concentration case is marked structural, yet its own prose contains a dated, timed event — Facebook's October 2021 BGP misconfiguration, “3.5 billion users offline for 6 hours”. The duration rule on the figure above is applied only to cases the corpus marks demonstrated, so that event gets no tick. Tense collapse happens inside single records too, not only across them.
  • Nothing records how sure anyone was. This atlas has no confidence field at all, so every case is presented at the same footing regardless of how well attested it is.
Audit · method

What is computed here

Three derivations, all forced by the same absence: this corpus has no time in it.

Event date — earliest source date, for the four cases the corpus marks demonstrated only. Duration — the longest interval matched by regex in a case's own prose. The still-running band — drawn for every structural case, and it asserts nothing except that the corpus records no end. The evidence window (2021-06-08 → 2025-12-01) is min/max of all citation dates.

severity is reproduced, never ordered. Its two values distinguish an evidential mode, not a magnitude, so nothing on this page sorts or colours by it as though it were a scale.

There is no map because this atlas's cases are global services and concentration facts. Placing “us-east-1” as a dot in Virginia would add a geography that carries almost none of the argument.
